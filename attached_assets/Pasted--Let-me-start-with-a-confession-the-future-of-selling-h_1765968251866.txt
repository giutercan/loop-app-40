“Let me start with a confession: ‘the future of selling’ has become a junk phrase. Everyone has a version of it, and none of it matters if we don’t understand the future of buying.

So the competitive advantage isn’t ‘who has AI.’ It’s who can build trust faster with evidence—and who can keep that trust through delivery.”

activity inflation—more content, more outputs, more noise

inconsistent execution—every rep does it differently

method decay under pressure—because training doesn’t show up in the moment

and buyer conversations that still feel generic—because the rep can’t translate all that AI output into evidence and a credible story

So from the CRO’s perspective, it looks like motion without lift.”

3:30–5:30 — The CRO’s real question (and why it’s not “deploy AI”)

“Here’s the CRO’s question in plain English:

‘I have the tech. I have the people. I need a step-change in win rate and revenue by 2027. What’s actually broken? Is it my GTM? My process? My seller behaviours? Or is it that my leadership system isn’t ready to run selling in the age of AI?’

Because the risk isn’t failing to deploy. The risk is deploying AI and automating the wrong thing—automating noise—then watching adoption stall and credibility drop.”

5:30–7:30 — Reframe: the unit of performance changed

“The old world ran on heroic sellers. The best sellers just ‘knew.’ Methods lived in training decks and memory.

The new world changes the unit of performance:
Seller + AI capability. Or more accurately: Team + AI capability, because this has to scale beyond heroics.

But AI only creates advantage if it’s doing specific jobs inside the workflow:

research assistant

pattern recogniser

method coach

story builder

follow-up engine

And the seller becomes the orchestrator of trust: judgement, influence, navigating complexity, closing.

That’s the promise. But there’s a missing piece.”

7:30–9:30 — The missing piece: proof (why value doesn’t compound)

“If you can’t prove what moved, you can’t scale it.

Most organisations can see activity, but they debate impact. They run interventions, but value proof fragments. Renewals and budget decisions depend too much on who tells the best story, not what the evidence shows.

And in the era of AI-powered buying, that’s fatal—because the buyer expects evidence. Credibility is earned, not claimed.”

(Beat)
“So the question becomes: what would a commercial system look like that improves outcomes and produces proof continuously?”

9:30–12:30 — The answer: Commercial Effectiveness 2.0 (Commercial OS) + KF Loop

“This is where Commercial Effectiveness 2.0 comes in: think of it as a Commercial Operating System.

It has three parts:

Part 1: A Success Frame (the scoreboard).
We lock 3–5 KPIs the CRO/CFO actually runs the business on:
win rate, deal size, new logos, cycle time, retention—and where relevant, cost of sale or critical talent attrition.

Not 50 metrics. A small scoreboard everyone agrees matters.

Part 2: Flow-of-work enablement (the behaviour engine).
AI becomes useful when it’s embedded in the moments that matter:
meeting prep, discovery planning, deal shaping, story structuring, follow-up, hygiene.

So method doesn’t decay under pressure because it’s reinforced in the moment—without adding admin.

Part 3: The closed loop (the evidence spine).
This is the part that turns activity into credibility:
Baseline → what changed → what shifted in behaviours/conditions → what moved in KPIs → new baseline → what we do next.

That’s the loop. It creates a continuous narrative that sponsors can defend and leaders can run.”

“We start with commercial effectiveness because it’s the fastest proving ground:

the metrics are clean

urgency is sponsor-owned

outcomes tie directly to revenue

and it forces the hardest discipline: one language, one method, embedded in workflow, with proof

Once that loop works, it becomes a template we can extend across other outcomes and lines of business as we align with KFI and the broader KPI libraries.”
