The core shift (this is the mental model)

The evidence pack is not a document.
It is the output of disciplined movement through the Loop.

So implementation means:

Evidence is generated as a by-product of work

Evidence is updated continuously

Evidence is re-used across sales, delivery, renewal

Evidence is structured for sponsor judgment, not KF storytelling

Think of the Loop as an evidence engine, not a sales tool.

1. Where the evidence pack lives in the Loop (structurally)

Inside the Loop, evidence sits in three fixed locations — never everywhere.

1️⃣ Success Frame (front of the Loop)

This is Evidence Definition, not measurement.

What becomes true here:

3–5 sponsor-owned KPIs

Baselines locked

Time horizon agreed

Explicit acknowledgment of uncertainty

Evidence artifact created:

Success Frame Snapshot
A one-screen view:

KPI

Baseline

Target / direction

Timeframe

Confidence level (High / Medium / Exploratory)

This is not KF-owned.
This is CRO + CFO owned.

2️⃣ Behaviour & Lever Trace (middle of the Loop)

This is the most important and most neglected layer.

Here, evidence answers:

“What did we deliberately try to change?”

You do not track everything.
You track chosen behavioural conditions.

Examples:

Deal shaping quality

Coaching cadence

Cross-seller collaboration

Use of solution tools

Decision clarity in buying group

Each condition is explicitly linked to:

The KF lever applied

Where it shows up in workflow

Which KPI(s) it is expected to influence

Evidence artifacts created:

Behavioural Condition Log (short, structured)

Lever → Behaviour → KPI hypothesis (one line each)

RAG status + narrative note (not explanation, interpretation)

This is where trust is built — because you’re not claiming certainty.

3️⃣ Outcome & Narrative Spine (end of the Loop)

This is Evidence Use, not reporting.

Here, evidence is shaped for:

QBRs

Renewals

Expansion conversations

ExCo updates

Evidence artifact created:

Sponsor Narrative Spine

A single, repeatable structure:

What we agreed success meant

Where we started

What we deliberately changed

What behaviours shifted (or didn’t)

What moved in the numbers

What we learned

What we will do next

No slides yet.
Just a spine that can become any artifact.

2. How evidence is generated (without adding admin)

This is critical.

Rule:
If evidence requires sellers or consultants to “fill something in”, adoption dies.

So evidence is generated through:

Workflow interactions

Decisions already being made

AI summarisation and patterning

Light confirmation, not creation

Practical implementation rules

Sellers select, not write
(choose KPIs, behaviours, levers from constrained sets)

AI summarises, humans sanction
(“Is this a fair reflection?” yes / adjust)

Evidence is event-triggered, not periodic
(deal stage change, QBR, renewal checkpoint)

Narrative is assembled, not authored
(the Loop stitches it together)

This aligns perfectly with your “AI as stabiliser of discipline” principle.

3. What must become true for the evidence pack to work

Anchor this to sponsor confidence.

For the CRO

For the CFO

For Sellers

Evidence helps them win and renew, not report

No duplicate admin

The story gets easier every time, not harder

4. Minimum viable evidence pack (inside the Loop)

If you do nothing else, implement only these four artefacts:

Success Frame Snapshot
(locked early, revisited quarterly)

Behavioural Condition Log
(3–5 conditions max, always explicit)

KPI Movement View
(baseline → current → trend, RAG + note)

Sponsor Narrative Spine
(auto-generated, reused everywhere)

That’s it.

Anything beyond this is optional until proof accumulates.

1. Reframe the evidence pack as a Role-Based Companion, not a document

The mistake to avoid:
One “evidence pack” that everyone looks at.

Instead, you design one underlying spine, with two lived experiences:

Seller persona:
“Help me move this deal forward without losing trust or discipline.”

Manager persona:
“Help me see where to intervene, what to reinforce, and what to stop.”

Same evidence.
Different lenses.
Same flow.

2. Seller persona: evidence as next-best sense-making
What the seller experiences (in flow)

The seller never thinks:

“I’m updating evidence.”

They experience:

“The system is helping me decide what to do next.”

So the evidence pack shows up as three lightweight surfaces in the seller’s flow.

A. “What matters right now” (situational awareness)

AI continuously answers for the seller:

What outcomes are we aiming at in this account?

Which stakeholders are tied to which outcomes?

What has already been agreed vs assumed?

This is Success Frame + context, not a report.

Evidence role:

Anchors the seller’s judgment

Prevents story drift

Keeps the seller aligned to sponsor language

B. “What’s missing or weak” (discipline under pressure)

This is where AI guides, not commands.

Examples of AI prompts:

“We have a target outcome, but no explicit behavioural condition agreed yet.”

“You’ve had three interactions with buying group members, but the economic buyer hasn’t validated success criteria.”

“This deal is progressing, but evidence linking your proposal to the agreed KPI hasn’t been captured.”

Evidence role:

Makes gaps visible before they become risks

Reinforces method without forcing it

Keeps trust intact under speed

C. “What should I do next?” (action guidance)

AI does not say:

“Do a blue sheet.”

It says:

“The next best move to strengthen confidence here is to align on X.”

“Deals like this typically stall when Y isn’t clarified — do you want to address it now?”

“Based on what’s changed, here are two credible next steps.”

Evidence role:

Converts learning into action

Protects seller judgment

Reduces cognitive load

The seller remains the orchestrator.
AI is the stabiliser.

3. Manager persona: evidence as intervention intelligence

Managers don’t need more dashboards.
They need clarity on where to lean in.

So for managers, the evidence pack shows up differently.

A. “Where is confidence breaking down?”

The manager sees:

Which deals lack an agreed Success Frame

Where behaviour change was targeted but not observed

Which accounts rely on narrative rather than evidence

This is pattern recognition, not micromanagement.

Evidence role:

Focuses manager attention

Prevents reactive coaching

Enables trust-based intervention

B. “Where should I coach vs intervene?”

AI guides the manager:

“This seller needs support on deal shaping.”

“This looks like a reward/process misalignment, not a skill issue.”

“This account is ready for executive involvement.”

Evidence role:

Separates performance problems from system problems

Stops managers defaulting to generic coaching

Makes leadership leverage explicit

C. “What’s scaling — and what’s noise?”

Over time, managers see:

Which behaviours actually correlate with KPI movement

Which plays repeat across accounts

Where KF levers are compounding vs stalling

Evidence role:

Turns local wins into reusable patterns

Creates managerial confidence to standardise

Feeds learning back into the system

This is how the Loop becomes organisational memory.

4. How AI makes it a living document (without admin)

The evidence pack must grow automatically as work happens.

Design rules (non-negotiable)

Evidence is inferred first, confirmed second
AI drafts → humans validate or adjust

Updates are event-triggered, not manual

Meeting held

Deal stage changed

Stakeholder added

Outcome discussed

QBR completed

Narrative accumulates, it doesn’t restart
Each loop adds:

What we tried

What shifted

What we learned

Nothing is ever “final”
Evidence is:

Versioned

Time-stamped

Contextual

This mirrors how real selling actually works.

3.2 Confidence scoring (simple v1)

Spine confidence increases when:

success frame is complete (+)

economic buyer validated items (+)

commitments are confirmed (+)

behavioral signals are observed (+)

proof objects attached (+)

Confidence decreases when:

stage advances with missing essentials (-)

contradictory items (-)

stale items (-)

risks unaddressed (-)

This enables “what’s missing/weak” guidance.
