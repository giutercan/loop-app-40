Executive summary

Most organisations converge on about seven enterprise OKR themes across industries: growth, profitability, customer, operations, people, digital and AI, and risk or sustainability.

You can codify these into a reusable OKR library with sector variants, then map client data to this library to infer their likely OKRs and strategies.

AI can scan leadership communications, plans and performance data to surface candidate objectives, key results and strategy patterns, then benchmark them against the library.

This creates a closed loop: inferred OKRs, aligned Korn Ferry interventions, measured outcomes and ROI, then refinement of both the OKR library and the client’s strategy.

The key decision is how much you standardise on a small set of OKR archetypes versus allowing free expression and using AI to translate that into structure.

Key decision / trade off
How far do you push clients to adopt a standard OKR pattern library, versus letting them describe goals in their own language and using AI to infer and align those goals to the standard library behind the scenes.

If you want speed to value, the pragmatic answer is a small standard library plus AI translation on top.

Why: what problem this solves

For your closed loop value capture story, you need three things:

A clear picture of what the client is trying to achieve in business terms.

A way to connect Korn Ferry services to those aims in a traceable way.

A data loop that shows movement in the client’s own metrics, not just proxy HR measures.

Standard OKR patterns plus AI inference give you:

A common language across Assess, Develop, Transform, Reward and Digital.

Faster onboarding of a client into the “loop” because you can start from patterns rather than a blank page.

Automated alignment checks between what leaders say, what the organisation measures and what people do.

This is your “conditions for success” box, and AI helps you populate it without weeks of interviews.

What: typical cross-industry organisation OKRs

Below is a compact OKR library you can reuse across industries. Think of these as archetypes that you then adapt by sector.

1. Growth and market position

Objective
Grow sustainable revenue and strengthen position in priority markets.

Example key results

Increase total revenue by X percent year on year.

Grow share in priority segment(s) by Y percentage points.

Launch N offers in strategic segments with at least Z adoption in year one.

Where it shows up in data
Board decks, investor reports, sales plans, product roadmaps.

2. Profitability and cost effectiveness

Objective
Improve profitability while protecting service and quality.

Example key results

Improve operating margin by X basis points.

Reduce unit cost to serve by Y percent in target channels.

Shift N percent of spend from run to change or growth initiatives.

Where it shows up
Finance packs, productivity programmes, procurement data, transformation cases.

3. Customer value and loyalty

Objective
Increase customer satisfaction and deepen relationships.

Example key results

Raise NPS or customer satisfaction score from A to B.

Improve customer retention from X percent to Y percent in priority segments.

Reduce complaint volume or response times by Z percent.

Where it shows up
Customer surveys, service dashboards, CRM.

4. Operational reliability and speed

Objective
Increase reliability, speed and resilience of core operations.

Example key results

Improve on time delivery or service levels from X to Y.

Reduce rework, error rates or incident volume by Z percent.

Improve cycle times for key processes by N percent.

Where it shows up
Operations dashboards, quality reports, risk logs.

5. People, leadership and culture

Objective
Build a capable, engaged workforce and AI ready leaders.

Example key results

Raise engagement or inclusion scores from X to Y in target groups.

Increase critical roles with ready successors from A percent to B percent.

Achieve N percent adoption of key AI tools by target personas.

Where it shows up
Engagement surveys, talent data, learning analytics, AI adoption reports.

Maps directly into KF Assess, Develop, Reward and Digital.

6. Digital, AI and innovation

Objective
Use data and AI to improve decisions, productivity and client outcomes.

Example key results

Deliver N AI use cases to production with measured impact on cost, revenue or risk.

Achieve X percent of transactions through digital or self service channels.

Reach Y percent of employees using AI assistance weekly in target workflows.

Where it shows up
Digital roadmaps, AI portfolio, product analytics.

7. Risk, compliance, sustainability

Objective
Manage risk and improve ESG and compliance performance.

Example key results

Reduce high severity incidents by X percent.

Achieve target ESG score or meet external standard by date D.

Reach N percent completion on priority compliance training with impact on observed behaviours.

Where it shows up
Risk reports, ESG reporting, internal audit, compliance dashboards.

You can treat these seven as your “master OKR set”, then define sector specific variants, for example:

Financial services: capital, liquidity, regulatory change.

Healthcare: clinical outcomes, patient safety, wait times.

Manufacturing: yield, scrap rate, safety incidents, OEE.

How: use AI to infer OKRs and strategies

Think in layers:

Library and ontology

Define your OKR archetypes and the metrics usually associated with each.

Link each archetype to Korn Ferry service lines. For example:

People and leadership OKRs → Assess, Develop, Reward.

Operating model and productivity OKRs → Transform.

AI adoption OKRs → Digital plus AI ready leadership offers.

Data sources to read
Prioritise sources that contain real strategy and intent, not only noise. For example:

Board and ExCo papers and packs.

Strategy decks, OKR docs, annual plans.

HR, finance, operations and customer dashboards.

Leadership town hall transcripts and internal comms.

AI extraction and mapping
Use models to do four core tasks:

Objective extraction

Find sentences with intent language such as “our aim”, “we will”, “target is”, “by 2027 we want”.

Metric and target extraction

Identify numbers, time frames and measures, for example “reduce attrition by 3 percent within 12 months”.

Topic and domain tagging

Classify each extracted objective into one or more archetypes, for example “customer”, “operations”, “growth”.

Source weighting

Give more weight to ExCo, board and formal plan documents than to casual channels.

Inferred OKR set
From this, the system can assemble a draft OKR map:

Top 3 enterprise objectives, labelled with archetypes.

Candidate key results with metric, baseline, target and timeframe.

Heatmap of where each OKR is most visible in the organisation, for example in HR data, in finance, in operations.

Strategy pattern recognition
On top of the OKRs, the model can identify strategy moves, for example:

“Shift to subscription revenue in segment X.”

“Automate back office process Y.”

“Build data products for external clients.”

These become links to Korn Ferry offerings and case stories.

Closed loop value capture
Finally, you connect this to the loop you and Jerry described.

Input: inferred OKRs and strategy patterns.

Process: KF interventions mapped to those OKRs.

Output: movement in the same metrics that defined the OKRs, plus ROI.

Feedback: update the OKR map to show which objectives and strategies are moving, and refine where KF has the strongest effect.
