1. What is client value realisation now?

In plain terms:

Client value realisation = tracking the actual, quantified outcomes a client gets from your work, against the value you promised, over time.

A few common threads in recent definitions:
	•	It’s explicitly quantified: business KPIs, not just “happy client” sentiment. Ecosystems’ definition is typical: tracking the quantified value a stakeholder receives from a solution and linking it to business impact and shareholder value.  ￼
	•	It is anchored to executive-level objectives – think OKRs / strategic pillars – not just project outputs. IBM frames value realisation as measuring impact on “executive-level business objectives” and tying it to a clear “North Star” using OKRs.  ￼
	•	It is a journey, not a post-project report: identify benefits, baseline, track, adjust, sustain – that’s the Benefits Realisation Management (BRM) pattern codified by PMI and others.  ￼

Your conversations with Jerry and Mirka are already in this territory: closed-loop input → process → output; conditions for success at the start; multiple loops; replication across the client’s business.  ￼  ￼

⸻

2. What are the big trends and what are clients actually asking for?

Trend 1: From “deliverables” to outcomes and shared KPIs
	•	Tech/services associations like TSIA describe a shift to “outcome-based, value-driven services” – engagements framed around business outcomes with contracts and service portfolios designed to deliver those outcomes.  ￼
	•	KPMG and others stress that “engineering your value realisation roadmap is as critical as implementing the capabilities” – value planning is now a core workstream, not an afterthought.  ￼

What clients are asking for here:
	•	“Don’t just deliver workshops / sprints – show me how this moves my OKRs.”
	•	Clear line of sight from intervention → operational metric → financial impact (e.g. time-to-fill → revenue at risk; leadership capability → productivity / risk).
	•	3–5 co-owned KPIs, not 30 vanity metrics.

This aligns tightly with your Value Lifecycle Platform concept of mapping discovery insights to strategic priorities and KPIs, then tracking them through the engagement.  ￼

⸻

Trend 2: Outcome-based and value-linked commercial models
	•	There’s a very explicit move (especially in tech and managed services) toward outcomes-based pricing and risk/reward sharing. TSIA and others highlight pricing models where payment is linked to business outcomes, not effort.  ￼
	•	Commentary on firms like McKinsey points to AI-enabled value verification enabling hybrid contracts where fees are linked to quantified impact (e.g. churn reduction, energy savings) because the data is now trackable.  ￼

Clients’ ask:
	•	“We want you to have skin in the game – but only if you can measure impact credibly and transparently.”
	•	CFOs in particular want payback period, NPV, and scenario analysis baked into the case, not just a slide labelled “ROI”.

Your SIOP ROI Estimation Process and examples are exactly the kind of pre-work firms are scaling – but others are now wiring this through into live tracking, not just pre-sale justification.  ￼

⸻

Trend 3: Institutionalising Value Realisation Offices and “value PMOs”
	•	Capgemini, PwC, Wavestone and others are now explicitly selling Value Realisation Offices (VROs) – AI-enabled teams and processes that connect strategy, delivery and benefits tracking across transformations.  ￼
	•	These VROs act as a central engine that keeps value on track: they own benefit registers, define KPIs, monitor progress, and feed insights back into portfolio decisions.

Clients’ ask:
	•	“Help us build the capability, not just the project.”
	•	Playbooks and tooling for benefits governance, not just project governance (who owns which metric, how it’s baselined, how often we review).

This is very close to your idea of a “closed loop value capture” system that can span multiple engagements and loops with a client.  ￼

⸻

Trend 4: Customer Success–style health scores, QBRs and value dashboards

SaaS and Customer Success are a few years ahead of most people businesses:
	•	Gainsight and others push customer health scores that blend leading indicators (adoption, engagement) with lagging ones (renewals, expansion, ROI).  ￼
	•	The 2024 Customer Success Index describes CS as a “strategic growth engine” focused on measurable ROI, expansion and AI-enhanced digital touchpoints.  ￼

Clients’ ask:
	•	“Don’t send us random status updates – give us a single view of health and value.”
	•	Structured QBR / EBR packs with a consistent story: where we started, what’s changed, what the data says, what we do next.

Your Value Lifecycle design already nods to this with KPI tracking, health scores and business review management as Phase 3 features.  ￼

⸻

Trend 5: Real-time, AI-assisted value tracking
	•	Multiple sources talk about real-time value tracking as the next step – using analytics to measure value continuously, not annually.  ￼
	•	AI is being used to:
	•	classify insights against value drivers,
	•	recommend KPIs, and
	•	detect early signals that benefits are at risk.  ￼

Clients’ ask:
	•	Faster time-to-insight: “Don’t make me wait three months for a benefits report; show me movement weekly/monthly.”
	•	Help to connect messy operational data to clear stories (“What is this doing to growth, cost, risk, talent?”).

Your platform concept – AI research, KPI recommendation, stakeholder narratives, financial engine – is directly in line with where the market is going.  ￼

⸻

Trend 6: People, HR and rewards value being pushed to hard numbers

In your space specifically:
	•	HR and benefits surveys (Aon, Mercer) show mounting pressure to link people investments to business performance, risk and resilience, not just engagement scores.  ￼
	•	Benefits and BRM literature stresses the need to quantify gains in productivity, retention and wellbeing in financial and risk terms, not just “culture”.  ￼

Clients’ ask:
	•	“If we invest in leaders, talent, org design or rewards – what’s the impact on margin, growth, risk and capability?”
	•	Benchmarks: “What does ‘good’ look like in my industry, and how far off are we?”

This is where Korn Ferry can differentiate harder than the generic value-realisation players, because you can tie talent levers directly to outcome patterns across Assess / Develop / Transform / Reward.

3. How are companies actually realising value?

Across the research, most “grown-up” value realisation setups follow a similar loop:
	1.	Define value up front
	•	Use a framework (Value Realisation, BRM, OKRs) to turn strategy into a small set of target outcomes.  ￼
	•	Create a benefits map: how specific initiatives (including people interventions) ladder to those outcomes.
	2.	Baseline and business case
	•	Quantify “today” (baseline) and “target” for each KPI.
	•	Build a financial case (NPV, payback, upside/downside scenarios).
	3.	Assign benefit owners
	•	Each benefit has a named owner on the client side; the provider co-owns methods and evidence.
	•	This is baked into governance, not treated as a finance exercise at year-end.  ￼
	4.	Track and intervene
	•	Use dashboards, health scores and QBRs – borrowed from Customer Success – to track progress, intervene early, and agree course corrections.  ￼
	5.	Codify and scale
	•	Convert successful patterns into templates and playbooks – value frameworks for specific solution types (e.g. SIOP, leadership pipelines, org redesign).
	•	Some firms wrap this in branded frameworks like Deloitte’s “Vision to Value” for ERP or IBM’s Value Realization for hybrid cloud.  ￼

Where they struggle:
	•	Data quality and access (especially for people/HR metrics).
	•	Maintaining focus on a few meaningful KPIs instead of bloated scorecards.
	•	Linking qualitative change (behaviour, culture, leadership) into hard value narratives.

That last piece is where Korn Ferry has an unfair advantage if you embrace it properly.

4. So what for Korn Ferry? How to simplify, innovate and change your own value realisation

You already have several ingredients:
	•	A clear strategic intent to build a Value Lifecycle Platform that joins discovery, alignment and realisation in one workspace, including KPI libraries and financial models.  ￼
	•	Existing ROI models (e.g. SIOP) that show how to quantify impact in specific offerings.  ￼
	•	A conceptual closed-loop value capture model (conditions for success → input → process → output → loops and replication) from your work with Jerry.  ￼
	•	The Mirka conversation highlighting the key design question: does this stop at “closed won”, or does it span active projects, backlog and ongoing value?  ￼

Here’s a way to turn the market trends into a simplified, KF-specific approach.

⸻

A. Simplify: standard, reusable value patterns

1. One simple value narrative across all service lines

Anchor everything on a small, shared value frame, e.g.:
	•	Grow (revenue, market share, innovation)
	•	Optimise (productivity, cost, efficiency)
	•	De-risk (turnover, critical role failure, compliance, reputation)
	•	Strengthen capability (leadership, culture, skills, diversity)

Every KF solution – Assess, Develop, Transform, Reward, Digital – hangs off one or more of these.

This gives sellers and consultants a common language to talk outcomes, and gives your platform a skeleton for templates.

2. Starter KPI packs, not encyclopaedias

You’ve already identified the need to start with 1–2 solution lines and small KPI libraries rather than boiling the ocean.  ￼

For those chosen lines (say: Sales Effectiveness + Leadership Development), define:
	•	5–10 leading indicators (behavioural / process metrics)
	•	5–10 lagging indicators (hard business results)
	•	For each: standard logic to link to financials (e.g. % improvement → £ impact bands)

This keeps it cognitively light for consultants and clients, but still defensible.

⸻

B. Innovate: design the platform and methods for multi-loop, multi-role value

Your current thinking about “a platform where sales, CSMs and professional services all see their own view on the same data” is exactly where the market is going; you’re just aiming it at consulting rather than SaaS.

So: treat the Value Lifecycle Platform as the operating system for value, not as a pre-sales tool.

Across your lifecycle flow (Discover & Qualify → Shape & Sell → Deliver & Realise → Review & Renew / Expand → Learn & Scale), build in:
	1.	Value Canvas at entry
	•	Standard template that captures: client North Star, 3–5 value outcomes, baseline, target, time horizon, and who owns each KPI on the client side.
	•	Pre-populated with KF IP for when client data is weak, as you and Jerry discussed.  ￼
	2.	Live “Value Board” during delivery
	•	Pull your ROI logic into a live board: each initiative has a value hypothesis, KPIs, owner and current status.
	•	Incorporate health scores (inspired by Customer Success) for each outcome: on track / at risk / off track.
	3.	Automated value stories at review points
	•	Use AI to generate:
	•	Exec summary (“Here’s how we’ve moved the dial on your top 3 priorities”)
	•	CFO annex (assumptions, deltas vs initial business case)
	•	People/HR story (behaviours, capability shifts and their contribution to outcomes)
	4.	Learning loop for KF itself
	•	Every engagement feeds anonymised patterns into your internal library: “For SIOP in industrial clients, these KPIs moved, by roughly this magnitude, over this timeframe.”
	•	This is exactly what others are doing inside VROs – but with your people-science twist.  ￼

⸻

C. Change: how Korn Ferry works around value realisation

Three concrete shifts to aim for:

1. Make value realisation a non-negotiable part of every engagement
	•	No major Assess / Develop / Transform programme without:
	•	A defined value canvas
	•	Named benefit owners on the client side
	•	Planned value review cadence (e.g. quarterly)
	•	You already have this philosophy in your FY26 roadmap (“Korn Ferry as Client 1”, internal case note, etc.); the trick is making it a policy, not a slide.  ￼

2. Align commercial models gradually with value maturity
Don’t jump straight to risky outcomes-based pricing everywhere. Use a ladder:
	•	Tier 1 – Evidence-rich fixed fee: traditional commercials, but with robust value tracking and case notes used for renewals and references.
	•	Tier 2 – Value-influenced: small success fees or bonuses linked to milestone outcomes (e.g. adoption, completion, early indicator movement).
	•	Tier 3 – Outcome-based pilots: only where data quality and control are high, and your platform can track impact credibly.

This mirrors what others are doing: using AI and data to underwrite more creative contracts, not as a gimmick.  ￼

3. Treat “Value Realisation” as a capability, not a project
You don’t necessarily need a full Value Realisation Office tomorrow, but you do need:
	•	A small central team (under Digital / Transformation) to curate KPI libraries, financial bridges and templates.
	•	Embedded “value leads” in key offerings who can shape how Assess / Develop / Transform / Reward express value in the shared framework.
	•	Explicit partnership with Finance to sign off on the ROI logic (so consultants aren’t inventing multipliers on the fly).

This is exactly the pattern in the VRO / BRM world – and your platform becomes the tooling for it.  ￼

⸻

5. Risks and trade-offs to keep in view

If you pitch this internally, leaders will (rightly) worry about:
	•	Data burden on consultants and clients
→ Your simplification lever is small KPI sets and automated data ingestion where possible.
	•	Over-promising impact
→ Use bands and scenarios, not false precision; keep Finance in the tent; pilot before you move to outcomes-based fees.
	•	Cultural resistance (“We already show value”)
→ Use storytelling plus hard numbers. Start with a few flagship client stories where you can show before/after KPI movement using the new approach.
	•	Tool vs behaviour
→ The platform will help, but the win is getting consultants to think in value loops. Training, playbooks and coaching need to reinforce that.

Given where you are with the Value Lifecycle work:

From there you’re not just “doing research”; you’re building the first proof points that Korn Ferry can be the firm that finally cracks closed-loop value realisation in the people and organisation space.
